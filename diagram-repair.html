DMCA §1201 Repair Exemptions — By Product Category

Diagnosis, maintenance, & repair classes (plus owner-data-access) across the nine triennial Copyright Office rulemakings, 2000–2024. Generated 2026-05-22.

This diagram remaps the §1201 record to product categories, because the codified § 201.40 classes don't track product types — one legal class (e.g. "consumer devices generally" in 2021) sweeps in phones, tablets, appliances, and other consumer goods all at once. Each cell shows what happened to that product category in that cycle: granted, renewed, expanded, narrowly carved out, proposed-and-denied, or absent from the record. Click a cell for the codified-class number, the Register's reasoning, and a verbatim citation.

"Granted" requires being part of an adopted Final Rule class. A product category can be "covered" without being named explicitly — e.g., tablets fall under the 2021 "consumer devices generally" class even though the text says nothing about tablets. Where that coverage required an inference, the cell flags it. Where a category was specifically requested and refused — commercial/industrial equipment (2021 & 2024), aircraft/avionics data (2018), game-console repair (2012 & 2015) — it's marked red. Repeat denials that eventually did succeed (the game-console optical-drive carve-out, finally granted in 2021 after two prior refusals) make the path-dependence of this regime visible.

Authority: only the Final Rule grants exemptions. Recommendations record the analysis. Comments and petitions are proposals only.

Scope. "Repair" here means classes whose adopted purpose is "diagnosis, maintenance, or repair" — vehicle/vessel, consumer-device, medical-device, food-prep-equipment, plus the 2024 vehicle/vessel owner-data-access class (repair-adjacent). Jailbreaking, unlocking, security research, and accessibility classes are NOT in this view (they appear in the companion diagram.html).

Note on inferred coverage. Where a product category is covered via a broader class without being named in the rule text (e.g., tablets, consumer PCs under "consumer devices generally"), the cell records the inference and the detail panel notes the underlying rationale. The codified test in 2021+ is just "primarily designed for use by consumers" — broad enough on its face to include a consumer laptop or Chromebook, even though the Register's analytical reasoning in the Recommendation emphasized characteristics (firmware "specifically created for a particular product") that fit embedded devices better than general-purpose computers. No Register opinion has squarely resolved PC coverage and no petitioner has clearly tested it; the cells reflect this as "plausibly covered, untested."

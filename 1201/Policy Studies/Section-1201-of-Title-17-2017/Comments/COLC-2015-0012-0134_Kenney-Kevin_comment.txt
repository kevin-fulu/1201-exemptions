Agricultural Engineering), I&#39;ve seen a transformation of Repair &amp; Diagnostics on the Farm go from Mechanical to Electronic.  This has rendered the Service Manuals (books) obsolete; which were made available just a few years ago to fix tractors and combines.  <br/>Today there is a purposeful &#39;Monopoly on Repair&#39; which prevents farmers &amp; ranchers like myself from fixing our own equipment.  We need software, not books; along with various userid&#39;s &amp; passwords just to find out what&#39;s wrong with our tractors &amp; combines we need to keep running.  <br/><br/>This is a bad deal for agriculture &amp; costs us a lot of time &amp; money which wasn&#39;t the case just a few years ago.  The added EPA Tier 4A Emission Requirements have made for additional difficulty repairing equipment in the field. My patent (Apparatus for reducing engine emissions utilizing multiple types of fuels-#8826888), can&#39;t possibly work with modern OEM&#39;s claims of copyright &amp; TPM&#39;s (technology protection measures) incorporated into their software on all makes/models of new tractors &amp; combines.  <br/><br/>In the LOC&#39;s Oct 2015 ruling, it says, &quot;...when circumvention is a necessary step undertaken by the authorized owner of the vehicle to allow the diagnosis, repair or lawful modification of a vehicle function&quot; (when referencing a &quot;mechanized agricultural vehicle&quot;-ie, Tractors and Combines we use in Nebraska)<br/>This exemption need to be made permanent, and must also be expanded to allow the owner of the equipment to utilize the services of independent technicians.   Without a permanent framework for interoperability and settings adjustments - innovations will be stifled. <br/><br/>

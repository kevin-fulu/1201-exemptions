Title: Additional Comments - Josephs, John
Posted: 2016-10-28T04:00:00Z
Submitter: Josephs-John

I am a consumer who has long been concerned about the provision of the DCMA prohibiting anyone from defeating limitations on accessing software.  Here are my comments:<br/><br/>Permanent Device Unlocking:<br/><br/>Yes, the exception should be permanent.  Locking a wireless device to a carrier does not protect the device, nor does it protect the owner of the device or the developer of the software it contains.  Locking merely enforces a contract between the carrier and the device owner and should automatically end with the contract.  In fact, if it is a crime to tamper with the lock during the contract period, then it should be a crime for the carrier not to unlock the device at the end of the contract period.  <br/><br/>Carriers have imposed two types of contractual periods:  purchase money lien equivalents and service changes.  Locks that are equivalent to a purchase money lien should be automatically released when the purchase money has been paid.  Many states impose penalties on creditors that fail to release a lien following full payment.  If the DCMA is going to allow itself to be used as a lien substitute, then it should incorporate similar consumer protections.  <br/><br/>Locks imposed as a condition for initiating new service on a fully-paid device should be disfavored, and certainly not extended or renewed with subsequent changes in service.<br/><br/>Computer Programs:<br/><br/>If I have purchased something I have the right to tinker with it, even if my tinkering renders it inoperable.  Manufacturers may justifiably refuse to provide warranty service if my tinkering caused the machine to fail.  This is true whether I tinkered with a machine&#39;s hardware or the software that runs it.  Why is it necessary for DCMA to limit access to the machine&#39;s software?  The only legitimate reason is to protect the software from being copied and sold, but copyright law provides penalties for that.  Another potential reason that I consider illegitimate is to eliminate competition for repair services.  The Copyright Office should not allow the DCMA to be used to support anticompetitive actions.<br/><br/>Therefore, there should be a permanent exemption for purposes of diagnosis, maintenance, and repair.  If feasible, this should be implemented immediately by the Copyright Office or Library of Congress as it is unrealistic to expect expeditious action by Congress.<br/><br/>Obsolete Technologies:<br/><br/>&quot;The rule follows where its reason leads; where the reason stops, there stops the rule.&quot;*  <br/><br/>Who or what is protected by restricting access to obsolete technology?<br/><br/>* K. Llewellyn, The Bramble Bush: &quot;On Our Law and its Study&quot; (Oceana Publications, New York, 1991), 189.<br/>

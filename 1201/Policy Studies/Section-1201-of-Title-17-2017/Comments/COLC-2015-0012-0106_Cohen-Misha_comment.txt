Comment ID: COLC-2015-0012-0106
Title: Additional Comments - Cohen, Misha
Posted: 2016-10-28T04:00:00Z
Submitter: Cohen-Misha

To Whom It May Concern:<br/><br/>In reading the Electronic Frontier Foundation&#39;s comments on this act, recommending &quot;strong, practical, and permanent exemptions to Section 1201 ... to protect repair ... from the threat of lawsuits and criminal prosecution,&quot; it strikes me that this is a very important aspect of the issue. To elaborate:<br/><br/>One easy way to reduce resource (including energy) use; pollution; and amount of garbage is to create products that can be repaired and/or upgraded (even indefinitely). If a block to this mindset of production is concern for and protection of intellectual property, that would be unfortunate and unintended. This would have to be worked around by exemptions or other means, so as to protect both inventors and encourage intelligent action.<br/><br/>To address a tangential issue: There is money to be made, in the short term, with planned obsolescence and with simply having customers discard old products and buy new ones. However, in the long run, the amount of money to be made from the infinite, succeeding generations of customers allowed by a preserved planet and longer-lasting resources, is much greater.<br/><br/>This kind of long-term thinking is especially in the purview of governments (that often consider timescales beyond a single human lifetime). But, even individuals may consider this when they think to posterity (that of their businesses and of their or others&#39; children).<br/><br/>This greater amount of money is apart from money that might be made by repair sub-fields.<br/><br/>In addition, repair fields might improve happiness by necessitating &quot;craftspeople.&quot; This complex, challenging, valued artisanal kind of work would replace and thus reduce reliance on perhaps less-humane, perhaps menial, factory labor.<br/><br/>Thank you for your consideration.

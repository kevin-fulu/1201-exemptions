I stand by the Free Software Foundation&#39;s submission to repeal the The Digital Millennium Copyright Act&#39;s (DMCA) anti-circumvention provisions.<br/><br/>An an American citizen living abroad, it is clear to me that the world is flat so far as access to information is concerned. My kids&#39; grandparents send them U.S. videos. The only way to watch them in my region is to circumvent the region encoding. Does that make my kids terrorists? The way the anti-circumvention provisions are written sure makes it sound that way, as does the Japanese version of such laws that were thrust upon Japan by the U.S. (The Japanese equivalent is called something like the &quot;Anti-Pirating and Anti-Terrorism Copyright Act.&quot;)<br/><br/>Citizens of the world deserve a more balanced copyright structure. The anti-circumvention provisions are an attempt to make illegal a grandparent sharing something they like with a grandchild.

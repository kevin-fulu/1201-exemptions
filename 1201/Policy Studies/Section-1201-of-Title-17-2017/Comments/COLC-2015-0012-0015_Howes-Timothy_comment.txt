Comment ID: COLC-2015-0012-0015
Title: Initial Comments -  Timothy Howes
Posted: 2016-03-04T05:00:00Z
Submitter: Howes-Timothy

The DMCA anti-circumvention provisions should be repealed, and we should no longer be forced to fight for piecemeal exemptions.<br/><br/>DRM systems do little to stop the illegitimate dissemination of copyrighted works. Instead, they are used to restrict the actions that legitimate owners may take with the devices and media that they have legally purchased. They are used to create &quot;lock-in&quot; that gives companies effective monopolies over people&#39;s software and media libraries. For example, people who purchase Amazon ebooks can never read them on any other devices, and Amazon can use their DRM to retroactively censor or remove books after purchase (as it memorably did by remotely deleting copies of &quot;1984&quot;). Similarly, people who purchase Android software from Google cannot switch to an Amazon device without re-purchasing the same software from a different store. DRM is anti-competitive.<br/><br/>The consequences of DRM are even more severe in the context of technical equipment. I work as a scientist, and it is important for scientists, doctors, and engineers to be able to have full control over the devices that perform essential work, such as microscopes, robotic equipment, and medical devices. In many cases, the software provided by the equipment manufacturer may be inadequate for a particular task, or it may have poor security, or it may be designed to create artificial vendor lock-in as described above. For the progress and transparency of science, it is important that we are able to replace the restricted and opaque software with customized code that can be shared with other groups and can be openly examined and understood.<br/><br/>The role of software in our daily lives continues to grow. I do not look forward to a future filled with cars that have their movements restricted, refrigerators that accept only certain brands of products, and medical devices that can be remotely monitored by insurance providers. Repeal these overreaching DMCA provisions, so that consumers have a chance to regain some of the autonomy that is gradually being eroded by technology companies.<br/>

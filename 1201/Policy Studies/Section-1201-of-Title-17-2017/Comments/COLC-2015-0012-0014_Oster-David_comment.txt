Comment ID: COLC-2015-0012-0014
Title: Initial Comments -  David Oster
Posted: 2016-03-04T05:00:00Z
Submitter: Oster-David

According to the U.S. Constitution, copyright exists to secure to creators a _limited_ right, with the _goal_ of increasing the works that can be used by the people of this country. The current copyright regime removes the limitations at the expense of the general public, the people that copyright was designed to benefit. DMCA exemptions don&#39;t fix the harm brought about by the DMCA&#39;s anti-circumvention provisions, but they&#39;re the only crumbs Congress deigned to throw us when they tossed out our rights as users.<br/><br/>Please change the rules so that exemptions, when renewed once, don&#39;t expire. Once we&#39;ve petitioned for an exemption for the DMCA&#39;s anti-circumvention provision and you&#39;ve had a chance to see its effect on the behavior on the economy, then we shouldn&#39;t have to petition again and again for the same exemption.<br/><br/>Every three years, supporters of user rights are forced to go through a Kafkaesque process fighting for exemptions from the anti-circumvention provisions of the DMCA.  Under the DMCA&#39;s rules, everything not permitted is forbidden. Unless we expend time and resources to protect and expand exemptions, users could be threatened with legal consequences for circumventing the DRM on their own devices and software and could face criminal penalties for sharing tools that allow others to do the same.

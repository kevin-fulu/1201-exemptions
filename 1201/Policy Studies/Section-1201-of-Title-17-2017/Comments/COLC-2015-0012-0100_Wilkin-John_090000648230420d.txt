This letter is provided in response to the September 27, 2016 notice of inquiry for 
further comment on the Section 1201 Study. Specifically, the University Library of the 
University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign wishes to comment on the proposal for a 
new permanent exemption for assistive technologies for use by persons who are blind, 
visually impaired, or print disabled. 
The University Library, one of the largest research libraries in North America, holds 
nearly 14 million print volumes and 24 million items and materials in all formats, 
languages, and subjects, including 9 million microforms, 120,000 serials, 148,000 audio­
recordings, over 930,000 audiovisual materials, over 280,000 electronic books, 12,000 
films, and 650,000 maps. 
The University Library has a strong commitment to providing services to individuals 
with disabilities. Many of these item in the University Library collection would, without 
some sort of technological assistance involving read aloud or screen reading 
technology, be inaccessible to individuals who are unable to read text in its originally 
produced format. 
The inclusion of a permanent exemption for assistive technologies for use by persons 
who are blind, visually impaired, or print disabled will better enable the University 
Library to provide services to all patrons, including those who need to disable

technological barriers to access in order to use a "read aloud" function or a "screen 
reader" or other "assistive technologies."1 
As has been noted in many of the comments by academic libraries provided to date, the 
University Library cannot utilize the "Library Exemption" to the DCMA for this 
purpose. Thus, such an exception is crucial to permit librarians to assist disabled 
patrons. 
Additionally, as noted in the recent WIPO Study on Copyright Limitations and 
Exceptions for the Visually Impaired, such a permanent exception to the DCMA would 
put the United States law in the company of 57 other nations whose laws include such 
an exception.2 
The permanent exemption would permit library patrons and librarians assisting them 
to avoid the confusing process of applying for a new exemption every three years as 
currently required in the DCMA provisions. Furthermore, it would permit academic 
libraries to better comply with the Americans with Disabilities Act requirement to 
provide auxiliary aids to accommodate disabled patrons.3 
We sincerely hope that this exemption will be made permanent and that our response 
to the call for comments has been helpful. Please do not hesitate to contact us if you 
have any additional questions.

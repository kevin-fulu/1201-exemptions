Comment ID: COLC-2015-0012-0069
Title: Initial Comments - Rico Robbins
Posted: 2016-03-04T05:00:00Z
Submitter: Robbins-Rico

General:<br/><span style='padding-left: 30px'></span>In my opinion, the anti-circumvention provisions laid out in the DMCA section 1201 is flawed.  The main problem the DMCA has tried to address, in both section 1201 and other sections, was to stop the spread of online piracy.  Make no mistake, I am not defending piracy by any means; however, from my perspective, people who pirate content just continue to find ways to circumvent technological measures anyway.  Additionally, by making circumvention a separate illegal act to copyright infringement, it makes otherwise legal activities illegal or legally risky.  Although there are a variety of exemptions that are in place, I believe it may not be enough.  As more technology and content becomes digital with digital control mechanisms, new and robust innovations protected by fair use may not happen because of the anti-circumvention provisions of the DMCA.<br/><span style='padding-left: 30px'></span>In terms of security, DMCA 1201 has been used to silence or chill security researchers in the past, such as with the Sony rootkit scandal.  Also, DMCA 1201 can be used to enforce terms of End User License Agreements, even if it&#39;s for anti-competitive purposes and circumvention the control does not inherently infringe copyright.<br/><br/>Rulemaking Process:<br/><span style='padding-left: 30px'></span>It is my belief that proposed exemptions should automatically renew.  Any time a temporary exemption should be revisited must require new evidence showing actual harm to copyright holders if the exemption in question continues to be implemented.  I fear a blanket allowing of exemptions should be revisited might allow copyright holders to continue to question existing exemptions every three years anyway, regardless of if there is any new evidence showing actual harm.<br/><br/>Anti-Trafficking Prohibitions:<br/><span style='padding-left: 30px'></span>In cases of circumvention of a technological measure that is allowed, not every end user has the means to do so with their own technical knowledge.  Distribution of tools may be able to help with that.  With that said, I believe Congress should act to allow the rulemaking process to equally extend to the anti-trafficking prohibitions, so long as it is not distributed with the aim of encouraging copyright infringing activities.<br/><br/>Permenent Exemptions:<br/><span style='padding-left: 30px'></span>I believe Congress should also act to allow permanent exemptions for the purposes of accessibility, making fair use of audiovisual works, and jailbreaking or rooting a mobile phone, tablet, etc.<br/><br/>Other:<br/><span style='padding-left: 30px'></span>As the Trans-Pacific Partnership&#39;s intellectual property chapter is modeled after existing copyright law, including the DMCA, proposed reforms may not be able to be implemented if the TPP is adapted in it&#39;s current form.  Furthermore, an ISDS dispute may ensue if such reforms are implemented into law by Congress.

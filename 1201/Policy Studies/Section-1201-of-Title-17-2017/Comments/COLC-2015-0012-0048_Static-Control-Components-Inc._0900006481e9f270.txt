Comments of Static Control Components, Inc.  
 Static Control Components, Inc. (“Static Control”) appreciates the opportunity to 
comment upon the issues posed by the Copyright Office in the § 1201 Study.  The issues 
addressed by the study permeate our field of printer supplies and components where the ability to 
interoperate and repair parts is paramount.  The Copyright Office requested comments in its 
Notice of Inquiry dated December 15, 2015 in the United States Federal Register, Volume 80, 
Number 249, pages 81369 – 81373 and extended the comment period in the United States 
Federal Register, Volume 81, Number 33, page 8545.  Static Control is focusing its comments on 
questions two, six, and seven posed by the Copyright Office. 
I. Summary  
 Static Control recommends legislative action to amend §1201 to state that repair and 
interoperability are allowed and lawful fair uses even if circumvention or copying is required to 
allow said repair or interoperability.  Static Control also recommends legislative action to amend 
§1201 to extend the exemptions for anti-circumvention to the anti-trafficking provisions. 
II. Background 
 Static Control began in 1987 and is headquartered in Sanford, North Carolina.  Static 
Control employs approximately 800 workers and manufactures and sells printer components and 
supplies, including microchips that it sells to third-party companies for use in the repair and 
remanufacture of toner and inkjet cartridges.  With almost 30 years of experience in the repair 
and remanufacturing industry Static Control is well suited to offer valuable insights and 
recommendations in response to the Copyright Office’s § 1201 questions.  Static Control also 
has extensive experience in complex litigation entailing questions of relating to § 1201 anti-
circumvention and reverse engineering, as well  patent, contract, anti-trust and false advertising 
issues.  
 To practice the lawful activity of repair, Static Control, and the repair industry as a 
whole, has encountered a “cloud of uncertainty [that] discourages perfectly reasonable and legal 
activity.”1 The repair industry includes many other companies like Static Control that sell

III. Comments on Interoperability and Anti-trafficking Provisions Posed by the § 1201 
Study (Questions 2, 6 and 7) 
 Static Control recommends Congress add language to § 1201 to clearly state that 
circumvention of access controls and technological protection measures (TPMs) to enable 
interoperability of products is an allowed fair use.  Static Control further seeks language that 
rejects purported contracts with end users that inhibit lawful interoperation as nothing more than 
masked business models of companies attempting to misuse the DMCA to “create monopolies of 
manufactured goods”.3   Currently, whether a specific circumvention falls under the doctrine of 
fair use or within the interoperability realm is a decision done on a case-by-case, court-by-court 
basis, which causes inconsistent results and stifles innovation.4   
 Section 1201 has been used by parties attempting to derive benefits not given by the 
DMCA.  One unsuccessful attempt to use the DMCA to prevent repair was Lexmark’s attempt to 
use access controls to enforce a non-copyright related business model that attempts to limit an 
end user’s right to repair or reuse a printer cartridge after purchase.5 Many companies similarly 
misuse TPMs and access controls in an attempt to protect an unprotected work. Concerns of such 
misuse of § 1201 to reach “anti-competitive goals” was echoed by the NTIA in its 
recommendations to the Registrar.6  
 Static Control wants to prevent more clever companies from devising similar tactics that 
improperly attempt to restrict repair and interoperation and reduce confidence in the copyright 
law.  This sentiment was echoed by the NTIA warning that such a “fundamental misuse of § 
1201 … can lead to the reduced respect for the DMCA and copyright law.”7   Therefore, Static 
Control respectfully requests that the language of § 1201 is amended to specifically heed the

warnings of the Sixth Circuit and NTIA in protecting against the use of the DMCA as an anti-
competitive weapon.  Companies should not be able to restrict interoperability or competition as 
they are necessary and lawful components for the advancement and innovation of intellectual 
property.   
 Two weapons used by companies intending to impose unlawful and anticompetitive 
restrictions are the anti-trafficking provisions §§ 1201(a)(2) and 1201(b).  The underlying 
requirement for each prong of liability in the anti-trafficking provisions is that the work must be 
“protected under this title”.8 Thus, the work must have copyright protection before any of these 
provisions may be applied.  However, as discussed above, business models that protect 
uncopyrightable work can be used in an anti-competitive ways to block interoperability and 
attempt to add rights not granted under intellectual property laws. The anti-trafficking provisions 
need to be amended to clarify that repair and interoperability are lawful fair uses even if 
circumvention or copying is required to allow said repair or interoperability. 
  In furtherance of these aims, Static Control believes that § 1201 should be amended to 
allow adoption of exemptions to the prohibition on circumvention and extend the exemptions to 
the anti-trafficking prohibitions.  As noted by the Librarian of Congress, he has no authority to 
adopt exemptions for the anti-trafficking prohibitions in §1201(a)(2) or (b) and therefore, 
congressional action and legislation is needed to extend such rights.9  Otherwise, a lawful act of 
creating and developing a work under one portion of §1201 would be barred from being 
manufactured, imported, or sold.10  Therefore, the extension of the exemptions to anti-trafficking 
provisions not only makes common sense, but will help to harmonize the current legislation.   
IV. Conclusion 
 Static Control respectfully requests that the Copyright Office recommend to Congress to 
amend §1201 to state that repair and interoperability are allowed and lawful fair uses even if 
circumvention or copying is required to allow said repair or interoperability and to extend the 
exemptions for anti-circumvention to the anti-trafficking provisions.

Title: Initial Comments - Brandon Anonymous
Posted: 2016-03-04T05:00:00Z
Submitter: Anonymous-Brandon

Topic: DRM removal on ebooks for fair use, in other words circumvention of technological measures for e-books.<br/><br/>Students already have the capacity to go to libraries, and photocopy books.<br/>They, or anyone, should be able to access legally obtained content for free, just as they already do with physical books.<br/>Access to information should not be limited after purchase, and if data is already stored on one&#39;s computer, it is their property. Computer data is composed of ones and zeros. Just as the configuration of patterns on a drawing board belong to the owner of the drawing board, the owner of the computer has the right to access information already on his/her computer, given that such information, i.e. textbooks in the form of ebooks, are merely non-tangible configurations on a hard-drive akin to drawings on an etch-a-sketch.<br/><br/>Digital rights management (DRM) schemes are various access control technologies that are used to restrict usage of proprietary hardware and copyrighted works.<br/><br/>The notion of intellectual property makes sense in regards to giving credit to authors, but it should not extend to the actual control over people as to what they can or cant do with software, or programs on their computers. The same can be said for mechanics who operate on their own cars.<br/><br/>The question is, who owns an e-book (configuration of &quot;scratches&quot; on a drive) on a computer?<br/>The computer owner, or the copyright holder? <br/><br/>Consider the concept of memory-rights management.<br/>Certain individuals, such as myself, posses a decent ability to remember things very well.<br/>We can memorize passages from books, and quote them from memory.<br/><br/>The law seems to distinguish from external memory(hard-drives and physical copies), and internal memory(of the human mind). DRM for e-books doesn&#39;t seem to work at all. It may close the front door on simply copying and pasting a file of a textbook, but it leaves the garage door wide open when one considers simple memorization techniques followed up by writing information down on pen and paper (or typing it up as one reads).<br/>If DRM exists for the purpose of denying the access to information (and in turn preventing the ability to transfer e-books between devices), and the law recognizes breaking DRM as illegal, then it ought to be illegal to remember things in one&#39;s own mind without special permissions as is the case with external memory devices.<br/><br/>Since it is not in the best interest of liberty to prevent people from remembering copyrighted information, it should not be in the interest of the government to deny citizens the ability to retain, or digitally transfer information from e-books that are legally obtained.  <br/>

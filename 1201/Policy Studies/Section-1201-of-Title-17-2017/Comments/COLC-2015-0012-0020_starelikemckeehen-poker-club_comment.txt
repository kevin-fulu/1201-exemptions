Title: Initial Comments - Starelikemckeehen Poker Club
Posted: 2016-03-04T05:00:00Z
Submitter: starelikemckeehen poker club

## Summary <br/><br/>I am submitting this comment as a professional game player and as a citizen of the United States. As a person who plays games professionally,  I object strongly to the way in which Section 1201 criminalizes my ability to effectively examine data and make use of gaming software or web-based software in ways that do not violate copyright law. Further, such restrictions put me at an economic disadvantage compared to gamers in countries with laws less strict than US law. Further, even if I wanted to, it is not feasible for me to seek exemptions for each platform or system that I use for which I would like to circumvent digital restrictions.  However, even if I were granted exemptions to each and every platform that I use for my job as a gamer, it would be of little practical purpose, because it would still be illegal for me to work with a community of software developers to create and improve the tools needed to circumvent access control technology. <br/><br/>## Comment<br/><br/>My reasons for wanting to circumvent access controls does not relate to any activity that would otherwise infringe upon a copyright holders works, however, the access controls I would be circumventing do also &quot;protect&quot; copyrighted materials. There are two primary reasons I want the ability to circumvent these access controls: <br/><br/>1) I wish to determine if personal and non-personal data is being tracked or transmitted to third parties. I want to know what information -- if any -- is being transmitted to third-parties. In many cases the game platforms do not say one way or another whether personally identifiable information is being tracked. However, circumventing the access control technologies to determine whether or not this is the case carries with it the risk that I would be committing a federal crime. This chicken-and-egg problem  seems to go against the spirit of Section 1201, which would allow me to, in a sense, otherwise prevent tracking of personal data if they were tracking and transmitting this data without my knowledge. But, I can&#39;t tell what is and what is not being transmitted without first  circumventing the encryption used on all the data and information being transmitted. <br/><br/>2) I wish to have access to game data and to make this data interoperable with other software tools taht I use to track and analyze such data. There is a lot of non-copyrighted data used in the game I play  and that is &quot;trapped&quot; behind digital restrictions. This data is valuable to me although there is little value of the data to anybody else and none of the data I seek would be subject to copyright. Gaming is a serious profession that can pay extremely well if you are better than your competitors. Being able to effectively log and track my gaming data is integral to whether or not a I -- or really any gamer -- can be successful at the highest levels. My competitors in other countries are able to circumvent digital restrictions and gain access to their data. I am not. This means that at the end of the day,  Section1201 puts me at an economic disadvantage over my competitors. <br/><br/>It is not feasible for me to seek an exemption for each gaming platform or system that I make use of as part of my profession. There are too many and the exemption process takes too much time and is too involved. However, I shouldn&#39;t need to get legal permission for non-infringing uses of my computer and data stored on my computing devices. If I am not committing copyright violation or violating any other laws (such as patent laws), then I should be permitted to change the state of computing devices I own without restriction. Ultimately, I want permission to use my hardware and to reconfigure data on that hardware without restriction so long as I am not violating copyright law or similar laws. <br/><br/>Lastly, there is no purpose in having exemptions without the freedom to create and share the tools to perform circumvention with others, including the public. I do not have the ability to create such tools on my own and it would only be feasible to do so with the help of others, especially the free software and open source software communities.

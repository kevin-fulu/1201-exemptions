# DMCA §1201 — 2027 Cycle Multi-Class Petition Plan

**Date:** 2026-05-23
**Cycle:** 10th triennial (NPRM mid-2026, Final Rule ~Oct 2027)
**Goal:** five new-class filings + renewals of all 2024 grants, with the long-term objective of §1201 repair exemptions covering every product category.
**Status:** planning document — needs coalition-lead sign-off per filing before evidence work begins.

This plan was developed in conversation with the §1201 archive (`analysis/lineages.json`, the nine Final Rules, and the Registers' Recommendations under `1201/`). It is not legal advice; the codified text in each Final Rule is the operative regulation.

---

## Executive summary

We file **five new-class petitions** plus **streamlined renewals** of every 2024-granted class. Each new-class filing is structured around a coherent user community + a concrete adverse-effects story, modeled on the closest existing class. Realistic outcome on the new asks: **2–4 grants, 1–3 narrowed grants, 1–2 record-builders for 2030**.

The 2024 Register denied "commercial and industrial equipment" broadly (Class 5 broader scope) and in 2021 (Class 12) for the same reason: insufficient record + non-cohesive class. Our work-around: **(a) split that bucket into cohesive user communities**, **(b) develop the record before filing, not after**, and **(c) frame each filing so the need is broad, the harm is narrow, and the additional scope does not expand copyright risk**. The Office's grants in 2024 (food-prep, medical-device, vehicle/vessel data) all turned on having concrete, named, documented use cases combined with a tight copyright posture. We need the equivalent in every filing.

**Filing structure after restructuring:**

| # | Filing | Frame |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Agricultural & Construction Equipment | Stationary ag (gap in (b)(13)) + mobile-construction clarification |
| 2 | Commercial-Service & Scientific Equipment (50/50 balanced) | Parallel sub-classes with identical TPM / copyright posture; broad need, narrow harm |
| 3 | American Re-industrialization: Manufacturing Equipment | Reshoring / factory-operator autonomy; 4 sub-asks |
| 4 | General-Purpose Computing & Networking | Closes the (b)(15) consumer-gate gap for commercial-use PCs / Macs / workstations; folds in enterprise IT |
| 5 | Building & Energy Infrastructure | Unified by "things attached to a commercial building / property" |

## Cycle calendar (anchored to the last four cycles' pattern)

| Date | Event | Our action |
|---|---|---|
| ~Jun–Jul 2026 | NPRM published | Stop scope changes; freeze filing list |
| ~Aug–Sep 2026 | Petitions due (~30–60 days post-NPRM) | File all 5 new-class petitions + renewal petitions |
| ~Dec 2026 | Round 1 (initial) comments due | **The deadline that matters** — full evidence record for each class |
| ~Mar 2027 | Round 2 (opposition) comments due | Triage which classes face opposition |
| ~Jun 2027 | Round 3 (reply) comments due | Reply on contested classes only |
| Summer 2027 | Public hearings (if Office calls them) | Witness prep for top 2–3 classes |
| ~Oct 2027 | Final Rule | Accept; use denials as 2030 record |

The hard deadline is **Round 1 (~Dec 2026)** — that's where the substantive evidence record is built. The petition itself is short. Plan the next 6 months around Round 1, not the petition.

## Filing 1 — Agricultural & Construction Equipment

### Scope
- **Stationary agricultural equipment** not constituting a motorized land vehicle: irrigation controllers; grain dryers and conditioning systems; milking robots and dairy automation; barn / livestock-feeding / ventilation automation; precision-ag base stations and field sensors; automated-greenhouse controllers; seed-conditioning equipment.
- **Stationary construction processing**: asphalt batch plants, concrete batch plants, aggregate processing, fixed crushing/screening.
- **Mobile construction equipment** — included as a clarifying ask: confirm that "motorized land vehicle" in (b)(13) reaches excavators, loaders, dozers, cranes-on-tracks, mobile pavers. Not a new class; a clarification on the record so OEMs stop reading the class narrowly.

### User community & political constituency
- Farmers, agricultural operators, ranchers, dairy operators.
- Construction-equipment owners (contractors, AGC members), independent service shops, equipment dealers (AED).
- Strong existing right-to-repair coalition; AFBF (Farm Bureau) and Repair Association already aligned.
- Bipartisan political surface (rural/red and right-to-repair/blue).

### Closest precedent
- 2024 §201.40(b)(13) motorized-land-vehicle class — established repair-class pattern; we are extending its logic to (a) stationary ag (gap) and (b) clarifying its reach to construction.
- 2024 §201.40(b)(14) vehicle/vessel data — useful for the data-access analog if needed.

### Draft class language (for refinement by counsel)
> Computer programs that are contained in and control the functioning of a lawfully acquired (i) agricultural machine or system that is not a motorized land vehicle, or (ii) stationary construction processing system, when circumvention is a necessary step to allow the diagnosis, maintenance, or repair of such a machine or system, and is not accomplished for the purpose of gaining access to other copyrighted works. "Maintenance" and "repair" carry the same meaning as in paragraph (b)(13).

### Evidence requirements
- 8+ named specific instances of TPM-induced repair failure (irrigation controller bricked at season; milking robot off-line; grain dryer locked during harvest; concrete-plant controller field replacement blocked).
- Economic harm quantified — downtime cost per day during planting/harvest/calving window; opportunity cost.
- OEM service unavailability or insufficiency — rural service-call delay statistics, parts-availability gaps.
- Non-circumventing alternatives — demonstrate none exist (or are inadequate).

### Witnesses & coalition leads
- **Primary coalition lead:** Repair Association.
- **Co-leads:** AFBF, AED (construction).
- **Witnesses:** 3+ farmers, 1+ dairy operator, 1+ construction-equipment owner, 1+ independent ag mechanic, 1+ aftermarket parts supplier.
- **Allied amici:** Public Knowledge, EFF, iFixit.

### Risk assessment
- **Low.** Strongest filing on the list. Stationary-ag is a clean gap in the existing vehicle class; the construction clarification is the Office's preferred posture (clarify rather than re-codify). Risk is being too greedy and including industrial-scale ag (mining-scale processing) that overlaps Filing 3.

### Expected outcome
- New (b)(N) class covering stationary ag + stationary construction processing.
- On-record clarification that (b)(13) reaches mobile construction.
- **Confidence: high.**

## Filing 2 — Commercial-Service & Scientific Equipment (50/50 balanced)

> **Two parallel sub-classes with identical TPM patterns, identical ISO-repair industry structures, and identical copyright posture** — the TPMs at issue (service-mode passwords, OEM calibration software, authenticated parts, encrypted firmware, phone-home license checks) gate device-control firmware, not access to creative works. Combining the two does not introduce a new copyright surface; existing §1201(f) (reverse engineering for interoperability) and §1201(j) (security testing) already provide the statutory baseline.
>
> **The combined case: the need is broad (every small commercial operator and every research lab), the harm is narrow (no creative-works content is at issue), and the additional scope of including both sub-classes does not expand copyright risk relative to either sub-class alone.**
>
> The scientific/lab sub-class has a distinct advantage on the §1201 record: the 2024 Class 5 proponents (Public Knowledge & iFixit, Initial Comment at PDF p. 9) explicitly excluded "devices designed or marketed for medical, scientific, or consumer use" from their petition. Scientific/lab is therefore a *deliberate carve-out, not a denial* — the §1201 record on it is clean and unopposed.

### Service-business scope
- Commercial fitness / wellness equipment (gym treadmills, strength machines, connected commercial cardio, group-fitness equipment).
- Commercial laundry (laundromat, hospitality, healthcare-laundry, dry-cleaning).
- Commercial cleaning (industrial vacuums, floor machines, pressure-wash equipment).
- Hospitality equipment beyond food-prep (commercial espresso machines, commercial dishwashers not in (b)(16), commercial garment care, hospitality minibar/vending).

### Scientific & lab scope
- Mass spectrometers and analytical chains (HPLC, UPLC, GC, GC-MS, LC-MS).
- Genomic / proteomic sequencers (Illumina, PacBio, Oxford Nanopore platforms).
- Electron microscopes (TEM, SEM, FIB, cryo-EM); confocal and super-resolution optical microscopes.
- Flow cytometers and cell sorters.
- Spectrophotometers (UV-Vis, FTIR, NMR), thermal cyclers / qPCR machines, ultracentrifuges, plate readers.
- Calibration, metrology, and electronic test & measurement equipment.

### User community
- **Service-business leg:** small-to-medium service businesses; the same profile that won the 2024 food-prep grant.
- **Scientific/lab leg:** research universities and core facilities, biotech and pharma R&D, contract research organizations, independent service organizations (ISOs) serving instrument repair. The user is institutional and budget-constrained — most lab managers depend on ISOs because OEM service contracts are 15–25% of instrument capex per year.
- Sympathetic Office posture: 2024 Register flagged "more developed record" as the reason food-prep got carved out — replicate that posture here, with scientific/lab as the most-developed sub-record.

### Closest precedent
- 2024 §201.40(b)(16) retail-level commercial food-preparation equipment — direct template for the service-business legs.
- 2024 §201.40(b)(17) medical devices — closest pattern for the lab leg: same ISO-repair industry structure, same "related data files" issue (calibration files, run-method files), same professional/institutional user posture.

### Draft class language
> Computer programs that are contained in and control the functioning of a lawfully acquired (i) retail-level commercial fitness, laundry, cleaning, or hospitality equipment; or (ii) scientific, laboratory, or research instrument, and related data files (including instrument calibration files, analytical method files, and run logs), when circumvention is a necessary step to allow the diagnosis, maintenance, or repair of such equipment or instrument, and is not accomplished for the purpose of gaining access to other copyrighted works.

### Evidence requirements
- McDonald's-ice-cream-equivalent stories for each sub-category. The 2024 grant turned on having a concrete, documented vendor-lock-in case. Replicate per sub-class.
- Specific named gym chains, laundromat operators, hospitality operators, lab managers with documented harms.
- **Lab-specific evidence:**
  - Service-contract cost differentials between OEM and independent ISOs (commonly 2–3× for the same scope of work).
  - Documented refusal-to-service by OEMs after end-of-life on instruments still in research use.
  - Downtime-cost impact on grant-funded research — NIH / NSF / DOE cycle delays, missed publication deadlines, lost graduate-student productivity.
  - Core-facility utilization records from research universities showing instrument-availability shortfalls.
  - OEM parts-availability gaps for instruments more than ~7 years old.
  - "Phone-home" authentication that bricks instruments without a manufacturer license check (specific named instrument lines with this behavior).

### Witnesses & coalition leads
- **Primary coalition lead:** Public Knowledge (carried 2024 food-prep) + Repair Association.
- **Co-leads (service):** NFIB (small business); independent gym, laundromat, and hospitality operator associations.
- **Co-leads (lab):** University procurement consortia (APLU / AAU member institutions); scientific-instrument ISO industry trade groups; research-society instrumentation interests (ACS analytical division; ASMS for mass-spec; ASCB / similar for microscopy).
- **Service-leg witnesses:** 2+ gym operators, 2+ laundromat operators, 1+ hotel facilities manager.
- **Lab-leg witnesses:** 2+ university core-facility directors (mass-spec, sequencing, and EM cores at separate institutions); 1+ biotech or pharma lab manager; 1+ scientific-instrument ISO repair company; 1+ NIH- or NSF-funded PI documenting research delay from equipment lockouts.
- **Allied amici:** SERI (sustainability — repair = less e-waste); university research-VP offices may write in support.

### Risk assessment
- **Low-medium.** Each leg is a direct analog of a 2024 grant. The 50/50 framing reinforces the "broad need, narrow harm, low additional copyright risk" argument: both legs face the same kind of TPM and neither involves access to creative works. The lab leg's additional advantage: the Office has not heard "no" on lab equipment specifically — the 2024 denial was for the broader-commercial bucket, which excluded lab by the proponents' own choice. The only structural risk: Office may codify as two paragraphs rather than one — pre-position with "alternatively, each as its own paragraph" fallback in the petition.

### Expected outcome
- One (b)(N) with separate paragraphs for commercial-service and scientific/lab, OR two adjacent (b)(N) classes. Either outcome is a win.
- **Confidence: high.**

## Filing 3 — American Re-industrialization: Manufacturing Equipment

> Anchored to the post-CHIPS-Act political moment: **domestic factory owners need autonomy over their own operations to ramp up American manufacturing.** Covers the full envelope of equipment a U.S. factory needs to operate — production lines, factory-floor automation, fab tooling, and manufacturing-facility infrastructure. Same substantive ask as the post-2024 industrial-automation strategy, *reframed around reshoring and operator sovereignty* and selectively expanded into adjacent fab/facility infrastructure.

### Political frame
- Reshoring, domestic manufacturing autonomy, and factory-operator sovereignty over their own equipment.
- Anchors to the CHIPS Act, the IRA's domestic-content provisions, and the bipartisan re-industrialization consensus.
- Reframes a twice-denied "industrial repair" ask into an "American competitiveness" ask — without changing the underlying copyright posture.
- Strategically: the reshoring framing may split NAM internally (large multinational OEMs vs. domestic mid-sized manufacturers who are NAM's growing constituency).

### Scope — 4 sub-asks
Filed as one petition (preferred) or four depending on Office preference:

- **3a · Regulated production lines.** PLCs and process controllers governing food / beverage / pharma manufacturing subject to FDA / FSMA documentation requirements. Strongest harm story: recall-traceability and audit requirements compound the lock-in cost.
- **3b · Abandoned-vendor / end-of-life industrial controllers.** Explicit class for equipment whose OEM no longer supports the product. Strongest copyright posture — vendor has exited the market, so there is no rights-holder to harm.
- **3c · Factory-floor automation.** Mid-sized factory robotics and motion controllers (assembly-line, palletizing, machine tending); industrial 3D printers (Stratasys, EOS, Markforged, HP); semiconductor-fab process tools; industrial test & measurement equipment used for production QA.
- **3d · Manufacturing facility infrastructure.** Industrial-scale HVAC for factory production environments, cleanroom HVAC and control systems, factory-scale power management. *Distinguished from Filing 5's commercial HVAC by user (factory operator vs. building owner) and purpose (production-environment process control vs. occupant comfort).*

### User community
- U.S. mid-sized manufacturers, in-house maintenance teams, third-party industrial service technicians.
- Reshoring beneficiaries: CHIPS-Act-funded semiconductor fabs, IRA-eligible clean-energy manufacturers, pharmaceutical-onshoring operators.
- Distinct from Filing 2 (small service-business + research labs), Filing 4 (general computing / enterprise IT), and Filing 5 (commercial buildings).

### Closest precedent
- 2024 §201.40(b)(17) medical devices — closest in the "professional/institutional repair under firmware control" pattern.
- 2024 §201.40(b)(21) 3D-printer feedstock — adjacent (does not reach device repair).
- **Adverse precedent:** 2021 Class 12 and 2024 Class 5 (broader scope) denials. The re-industrialization framing is the strategic response to those denials.

### Draft class language (4 paragraphs)
> (i) Computer programs that are contained in and control the functioning of programmable logic controllers operating commercial packaging, filling, or process equipment subject to recordkeeping requirements under the Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act or the Food Safety Modernization Act, when circumvention is a necessary step to allow the diagnosis, maintenance, or repair of such equipment.
>
> (ii) Computer programs that are contained in and control the functioning of any lawfully acquired industrial control system or automation equipment for which the manufacturer has discontinued support and replacement parts are not commercially available, when circumvention is a necessary step to allow the diagnosis, maintenance, or repair of such equipment.
>
> (iii) Computer programs that are contained in and control the functioning of a lawfully acquired industrial 3D printer, factory robot, motion controller, semiconductor-fabrication process tool, or production test and measurement instrument, when circumvention is a necessary step to allow the diagnosis, maintenance, or repair of such equipment.
>
> (iv) Computer programs that are contained in and control the functioning of an industrial-scale heating, ventilation, air-conditioning, cleanroom, or facility-power-management system that is used to maintain the production environment of a manufacturing facility, when circumvention is a necessary step to allow the diagnosis, maintenance, or repair of such a system.

### Evidence requirements
- For 3a (regulated production): named pharma / food-processor case studies; FDA-traceability impact analysis; reasons existing vendor-only service is inadequate.
- For 3b (abandoned vendors): catalog of significantly-installed industrial automation product lines whose OEM has discontinued support; documented orphan-equipment population.
- For 3c (factory floor): mid-size manufacturer testimony; refusal-to-service data; ROI of repair vs. replacement; AMUG-collected service-failure stories for 3D printers; semiconductor-fab tool downtime impact on CHIPS-Act-funded fab ramp-up.
- For 3d (facility infrastructure): documented industrial-HVAC lockouts in production facilities; cleanroom-system bricking incidents in biotech and semiconductor manufacturing.

### Witnesses & coalition leads
- **Primary coalition lead:** Repair Association industrial track + Reshoring Initiative.
- **Co-leads:** SMRP (Maintenance & Reliability Professionals); AMUG (additive manufacturing); manufacturing-policy think tanks aligned with the post-CHIPS-Act agenda; mid-sized manufacturer associations (SME, NTMA); semiconductor-equipment user groups for the fab-tool sub-leg.
- **Witnesses:** 2+ pharma / food-processor maintenance managers; 2+ manufacturers with orphaned automation; 1+ industrial 3D-print service bureau; 1+ university makerspace with industrial equipment; 1+ CHIPS-Act-funded fab maintenance director (or analogous reshoring manufacturer).
- **Opposition to expect:** PMMI, NAM (mixed — see political frame above), individual OEMs (Stratasys, ABB, Rockwell, Siemens-aligned voices, Applied Materials, Lam Research for fab tools).

### Risk assessment
- **Medium-high.** Office has denied this bucket twice; the re-industrialization framing is the strategic response, not a guarantee. Four sub-asks give multiple shots. **3b (abandoned vendor) remains the most likely to win** because copyright concern is weakest when the vendor has exited. **3d (facility infrastructure) is the highest-risk sub-ask** — most novel and most opposed.

### Expected outcome
- 1–2 of 4 sub-asks granted (most likely 3b abandoned-vendor and either 3a regulated-pharma or 3c factory-floor).
- Other sub-asks build the 2030 record.
- The political frame may produce sympathetic Register dicta even where the scope is denied — useful for the 2030 cycle.
- **Confidence: medium-low.**

## Filing 4 — General-Purpose Computing & Networking

> Hardware repair under firmware lockout across the full general-purpose computing stack — from consumer-grade laptops used commercially through professional workstations through datacenter hardware. **The (b)(15) consumer-device class's "primarily designed for use by consumers" gate is genuinely ambiguous for PCs / Macs / laptops used in commercial settings — this filing closes that gap explicitly**, and folds enterprise IT in alongside it under a unified "hardware repair, not software access" framing.

### Scope — general-purpose computing
- Consumer-grade computers in commercial use — laptops, desktops, all-in-ones used by businesses, schools, healthcare practices, government agencies, nonprofits.
- Professional workstations — Apple Mac Pro / Mac Studio, Dell Precision, HP Z-series, Lenovo ThinkStation. Clearly outside (b)(15)'s "consumer" gate by design and marketing.
- Apple Silicon parts-pairing across the Mac line — T2 / Secure Enclave authentication; locked SSDs and Touch-ID parts; soldered RAM with brick-on-replacement behavior.
- ChromeOS and Windows-Hello hardware bindings; TPM-anchored boot lockouts after motherboard replacement.

### Scope — networking & datacenter
- Servers and server boards (CPU, memory, storage, NIC replacement).
- Network switches, routers, optical/transport gear (port-level component replacement, fan/PSU swap).
- Storage arrays (SSD/HDD replacement, controller card replacement).
- UPS / PDU / datacenter power equipment (battery, board, monitoring-card replacement).
- Datacenter environmental units (CRAC/CRAH controllers, in-row cooling).

### Critical framing — narrowest scope wins
- Petition is for **hardware repair under firmware lockout** only.
- Explicitly **excludes**:
  - Bypassing software entitlements / per-port / per-VM / capacity-based licensing.
  - Modifying or accessing the OS, hypervisor, or management-plane software beyond what is strictly necessary for hardware component substitution.
  - Defeating subscription gates on software features or DRM-protected content (FairPlay, Widevine, streaming-service DRM).
- This is the lesson from 2024: broad framings on computing lose because the Office sees software-licensing or content-DRM concerns. Carving narrowly to hardware-repair-only changes the legal posture.

### User community & political constituency
- **Commercial computing leg:** businesses, schools, healthcare practices, government agencies, nonprofits using PCs / Macs / laptops / workstations; school IT directors; small-business IT operators; MSPs and managed-IT providers.
- **Enterprise IT leg:** enterprise IT operators, MSPs, hyperscaler secondary-market refurbishers, sustainability/e-waste-reduction advocates.
- Distinct user from any other filing.

### Why now
- **(b)(15) gap on commercial use:** (b)(15) reaches devices "primarily designed for use by consumers." Commercial-use PCs and workstations sit outside that gate by both design intent and marketing.
- **2012 SFLC denial is stale:** Only PC-repair petition ever filed (2012, denied for evidentiary thinness). That denial is 13 years old; the landscape has changed completely — Apple Silicon parts-pairing, locked SSDs, T2 / secure-enclave authentication, surface bonding.
- **Refurbisher data is now mature:** SERI, ITRH, e-waste-diversion impact at scale.

### Closest precedent
- 2024 §201.40(b)(15) consumer-device repair — analogous for non-commercial PCs; this filing extends the same logic to commercial use of the same hardware.
- 2024 §201.40(b)(17) medical devices — closest "professional repair under firmware control, including related data files" template for the enterprise IT leg.

### Draft class language (2 paragraphs)
> (i) Computer programs that are contained in and control the functioning of a lawfully acquired general-purpose computing device — including desktops, laptops, all-in-ones, and professional workstations — when the device is used in a commercial, educational, governmental, or nonprofit setting, and when circumvention is a necessary step to allow the diagnosis, maintenance, or repair of such a device.
>
> (ii) Computer programs that are contained in and control the functioning of a lawfully acquired enterprise computing or networking device — including servers, network switches, routers, storage arrays, uninterruptible power supplies, and datacenter environmental equipment — when circumvention is a necessary step to allow the diagnosis, maintenance, or repair of such a device.
>
> In each case: "Diagnosis, maintenance, or repair" is limited to the restoration of the device or its components to working in accordance with the device's original specifications; circumvention is not accomplished for the purpose of bypassing software licensing, entitlement, or subscription mechanisms, or for accessing operating-system, management-plane, or DRM-protected content beyond what is necessary for hardware component replacement; and any technological measures circumvented are restored to their original state to the extent reasonably practicable upon completion of the repair.

### Evidence requirements
- **Commercial-computing leg:** specific named cases of Apple Silicon parts-pairing blocking repair; documented business / school / nonprofit lockouts; documented Apple repair-program denials for commercial users; ChromeOS and Windows-Hello hardware-binding cases.
- **Enterprise leg:** refurbisher-industry data — market size, e-waste-diversion impact, specific equipment categories blocked; specific named cases (storage-array PSU swap blocked by chassis firmware; switch port-locked PSU; server board with TPM checks on component replacement); sustainability impact quantified.
- Explicit non-impact on software licensing or content DRM — show concrete examples that the petition's scope does NOT include those concerns.

### Witnesses & coalition leads
- **Primary coalition lead:** Repair Association IT / PC track + SERI (sustainability) or ITRH (IT-refurbisher industry).
- **Co-leads:** EFF; nonprofit IT-refurbisher consortia; commercial-PC user communities (school IT, small-business IT, nonprofit / philanthropy IT); MSPs and managed-IT trade associations.
- **Commercial-computing witnesses:** 2+ school IT directors; 1+ small-business IT operator; 1+ MSP serving SMB / nonprofit clients; 1+ independent Mac repair shop with commercial customers.
- **Enterprise witnesses:** 2+ enterprise refurbishers (Sims, ITRenew analogs); 1+ MSP; 1+ sustainability / circular-economy advocate; 1+ enterprise IT director willing to testify about lock-in.
- **Opposition:** Apple (most aggressive — has fought repair exemptions across multiple cycles), Microsoft, HPE, Dell, Cisco, NetApp, Lenovo. **Strongest opposition of any filing.**

### Risk assessment
- **High for full scope. Medium for a commercial-PC + workstation carve-out** (clearly outside (b)(15)'s consumer gate). The narrow framing — hardware repair only, no licensing / OS access / content-DRM bypass — is essential. Apple will fight every variant. Strategy: file knowing the full scope likely gets denied, but the carve-outs and the on-record record build the 2030 case.

### Expected outcome
- **Likely partial grant.** Strongest case: **professional workstations** (Mac Pro, Dell Precision, HP Z) — clearly commercial by design.
- Medium case: **commercial-use laptops / desktops** (Office may say (b)(15) already covers and decline to extend).
- Datacenter / enterprise gear: likely denied at full scope; narrow **abandoned-vendor IT carve-out** plausible (parallel to Filing 3b).
- **Confidence: medium-low for full grant; medium-high for workstation + carve-outs; high for record-building.**

## Filing 5 — Building & Energy Infrastructure

> Repair of equipment attached to commercial buildings or to a property's energy / charging infrastructure. **Unified user community: building owners, facility managers, and energy operators** — distinct from Filing 2's small-commercial-service operators and Filing 3's factory operators. Buildings have HVAC, BMS, energy generation/storage, and EV charging; this filing groups them by "where they live" rather than by individual equipment category.

### Building scope
- Commercial HVAC — rooftop units, chillers, VRF systems, walk-in refrigeration, commercial-scale heating/cooling.
- Building management systems (BMS) and building automation controllers.
- Commercial elevator and access controllers — with life-safety carve-out (below).
- *Excludes Filing 3's industrial-scale factory HVAC (different user, different purpose) and the residential HVAC already reached by (b)(15)'s home-systems leg.*

### Energy scope
- Solar inverters and microinverter systems (Enphase, SolarEdge, SMA, Fronius, GoodWe).
- Commercial battery energy-storage systems (Tesla Powerwall- and Megapack-scale, LG, Enphase IQ, EG4).
- Energy management systems (EMS); microgrid controllers.
- EV charging infrastructure — Level 2 and DC fast chargers in commercial, fleet, multi-family, and workplace settings.

### Critical framing — safety
- Office will worry about grid-interconnection and life-safety. The class must:
  - Limit repair to restoration of original specifications.
  - Exclude any modification that changes the equipment's grid-interconnect behavior, safety setpoints, or certified parameters (UL 1741, IEEE 1547).
  - Carve out life-safety systems entirely (fire alarm, smoke control, intrusion detection, elevator interlock, sprinkler controls).
  - Carve out modifications that affect HVAC's regulated efficiency or emissions parameters (EPA, DOE).
- Without these carve-outs, this filing is denied for safety reasons regardless of merits.

### User community
- Commercial property owners, BOMA members, IFMA members, facilities managers.
- Energy operators, EV-charging network operators, solar installers servicing commercial accounts.
- Distinct from Filing 2 (small commercial operators in the *service* business) and Filing 3 (factory operators). The differentiator: this user owns or manages a *building or property*, not a production line or a service business.

### Closest precedent
- 2024 §201.40(b)(13) vehicle/vessel — vehicles have safety-critical components and the Office has accepted "restore original specifications" repair.
- 2024 §201.40(b)(17) medical devices — for the "professional repair, related data files included" pattern.
- 2024 §201.40(b)(15) consumer-devices home-systems leg — analogous for the residential side of the same equipment categories (this filing extends the same logic to the commercial side).

### Draft class language (2 paragraphs)
> (i) Computer programs that are contained in and control the functioning of a lawfully acquired energy-generation, energy-storage, energy-management, or electric-vehicle-charging system installed at a commercial, multi-family, or institutional property — including grid-tied solar inverters, commercial battery energy-storage systems, microgrid controllers, and Level 2 or DC fast EV chargers — when circumvention is a necessary step to allow the diagnosis, maintenance, or repair of such a system.
>
> (ii) Computer programs that are contained in and control the functioning of a lawfully acquired commercial heating, ventilation, air-conditioning, refrigeration, building management, or building-automation system, other than a fire, security, or life-safety system, when circumvention is a necessary step to allow the diagnosis, maintenance, or repair of such a system.
>
> In each case: such circumvention shall not modify the system's certified grid-interconnect behavior (UL 1741, IEEE 1547), its life-safety parameters, or its regulated efficiency or emissions parameters (EPA, DOE); and any technological measures circumvented are restored to their original state to the extent reasonably practicable upon completion of the repair.

### Evidence requirements
- **Building leg:** documented commercial-HVAC service-exclusivity by Carrier / Trane / Daikin / Honeywell / Johnson Controls; BOMA-member building-owner testimony on lock-out incidents; BMS bricking and orphan-vendor cases.
- **Energy leg:** SunPower bankruptcy aftermath (tens of thousands of homeowners with locked monitoring); EV-charger network failures (ChargePoint, EVgo, Blink); documented solar-inverter and battery field failures with vendor-only-repair behavior.
- Repair-industry economic data: solar-installer service-call patterns, EV-charger uptime statistics, commercial-HVAC service-contract pricing.
- Grid-impact analysis: demonstrate that "restore original specifications" repair does not affect interconnection compliance.
- Safety-system carve-out: explicit demonstration of what is in / out of scope.

### Witnesses & coalition leads
- **Primary coalition lead:** BOMA (Building Owners and Managers Association) + SEIA (Solar Energy Industries Association).
- **Co-leads:** IFMA (International Facility Management Association); ChargePoint and fleet-operator coalitions; local-solar advocates (Solar United Neighbors); IREC (Interstate Renewable Energy Council).
- **Building witnesses:** 2+ commercial-property facilities managers; 1+ BMS service technician at a non-OEM service company; 1+ commercial HVAC technician.
- **Energy witnesses:** 2+ solar installers with abandoned-equipment cases; 2+ EV-charger operators (including at least one fleet operator); 1+ grid-safety engineer (to validate the safety-framing).
- **Opposition:** Tesla (EV chargers + solar + batteries combined), Enphase, SolarEdge; Carrier, Trane, Daikin, Honeywell, Johnson Controls (commercial HVAC). ChargePoint may be opposition or co-lead depending on case selection.

### Risk assessment
- **Medium-high.** The unified "building infrastructure" user community is more cohesive than the prior building/energy split. Safety-framing is the make-or-break. Concrete bricking incidents (SunPower bankruptcy, EV-charger network outages) are the strongest wedge. Splitting building from energy is a fallback if the Office prefers narrower scope.

### Expected outcome
- **EV charging** most likely granted (clearest concrete record, smallest opposition).
- **Commercial HVAC** plausibly granted with life-safety carve-out.
- **Solar / battery** mixed (heavy vendor opposition).
- **BMS** tricky (safety-system overlap).
- **Confidence: medium.**

## Phased action plan

### Phase 1 — Scope confirmation & coalition assembly (now → end of Q3 2026)
**Deadline: Sep 2026 (when NPRM lands).**
1. Confirm final filing scope (this document).
2. Recruit a named coalition lead per filing (5 named individuals/orgs).
3. Sign an evidence-intake commitment with each coalition.
4. Open a shared evidence-collection workspace (the rest of this plan rolls into it).

### Phase 2 — Evidence development (Q2 2026 → Q4 2026)
**Deadline: Round 1 comment date (~Dec 2026).**
1. Per-filing evidence brief: 5–10 pages each, with specific named instances, economic harm, OEM service-availability data, and absence-of-alternatives analysis.
2. Recruit witnesses (minimum 2 per filing, target 4+).
3. Get pre-commitment letters from coalition partners.
4. Begin draft Round 1 comment per filing in parallel.

### Phase 3 — Petition drafting & filing (Q3 2026, on the NPRM clock)
**Deadline: ~30–60 days after NPRM (~Aug–Sep 2026).**
1. Draft 5 petitions modeled on the closest existing class language (drafts above).
2. Coordinate with allies — multiple petitioners on each class is fine; pre-arrange a lead so the Office doesn't have to consolidate.
3. File renewal petitions as a single batch.

### Phase 4 — Round 1 comments (~Dec 2026)
**The critical deadline.** Each filing gets its long-form initial comment with the Phase 2 evidence. Each comment should:
- Lead with the concrete, named-instance harms.
- Show non-circumventing alternatives are inadequate.
- Show the proposed exemption is no broader than necessary.
- Address known opposition arguments preemptively.

### Phase 5 — Round 2 & 3 + hearings (Q1 → Q3 2027)
1. Read all Round 2 (opposition) filings; triage which classes need reply.
2. Round 3 (reply) — written response to opposition arguments. Focus on classes with substantive opposition; let uncontested ones coast.
3. Prepare for hearings (if Office calls them) — typically the Office picks the most-contested classes for hearings. Expect Filings 3, 4, and possibly 5 to be called.

### Phase 6 — Final Rule (~Oct 2027)
1. Document the outcomes per filing.
2. For denials: pull the Register's reasoning and convert into 2030-cycle TODOs (evidence gaps to fill, witnesses to add, class-language refinements).

DMCA §1201 Anticircumvention Exemptions & Denied Petitions — Cycle-by-Cycle History

Every class that has won an exemption, plus the substantively-significant proposals that were denied, across nine triennial U.S. Copyright Office §1201 rulemakings (2000–2024). Generated 2026-05-22.

Rows are granted exemptions (regular text) and denied petitions (italic, muted). Both are grouped by equipment/work category. Columns are the nine triennial cycles in chronological order. Each cell shows what happened to that proposal in that cycle, color-coded by status. Matching across cycles is by substance, not by class number.

A red triangle in the top-right corner of any cell indicates a partial denial within that cycle — typically a proposed scope expansion that was rejected even though the underlying class was renewed or granted. On desktop, hover any cell for cross-cycle context; on mobile or in print, scroll to the Per-exemption details section below the matrix, which contains every per-cycle note in expandable form.

Authority. Only the Final Rule grants exemptions. Recommendations are advisory; comments are proposals. Denials cited here all come from the Final Rule (or, where the Final Rule is silent, the Register's Recommendation) — every per-cycle JSON in analysis/ carries the supporting [PAGE N] citation.

Filtering & Internet Content

Filtering software block-lists first granted 2000

Circumvent TPMs on lists of websites blocked by content-filtering software.

  • 2000 (b)(1) — new (first granted) — one of two classes granted in the very first §1201 rulemaking
  • 2003 (b)(1) — renewed unchanged — refined to "lists of Internet locations blocked"
  • 2006 — dropped — renewal petition (B.1) denied for lack of factual showing
  • 2010–2024 — not renewed

The original filtering-software research justification was overtaken by broader content-filter debate.

Legacy Access Controls (Obsolete or Broken)

Literary works with malfunctioning/obsolete access controls first granted 2000

Broad first-cycle grant covering broken/obsolete TPMs on literary works incl. programs and databases.

  • 2000 (b)(2) — new (first granted)
  • 2003+ — replaced by narrower successor classes (obsolete dongles + obsolete-format programs)
Obsolete dongles first granted 2003 · last renewed 2010

Circumvent obsolete/broken hardware dongles guarding lawfully obtained computer programs.

  • 2003 (b)(2) — new
  • 2006 (b)(3) — renewed unchanged — partial denial: a request to expand the class to broader incompatibilities/obsolete OS or hardware was rejected as procedurally untimely (made for the first time at the hearing)
  • 2010 (b)(5) — renewed unchanged — embedded dongle-obsolescence definition retained
  • 2012+ — not renewed; legacy dongle problem became historical
Computer programs/games in obsolete formats first granted 2003 · last renewed 2006

Programs/games in obsolete formats. Narrowed in 2006 to LAM preservation contexts.

  • 2003 (b)(3) — new
  • 2006 (b)(2) — renewed narrowed — refined to library/archive preservation purposes
  • 2010+ — not renewed; conceptually revived as the separately-tracked software-preservation-lams class in 2018

Accessibility

Literary works (electronic) — accessibility (musical notation added 2021) first granted 2003 · 8 consecutive cycles

Circumvent TPMs that block read-aloud / interfere with screen readers, for blind/disabled users or authorized entities under 17 U.S.C. 121.

  • 2003 (b)(4) — new
  • 2006 (b)(4), 2010 (b)(6), 2012 (b)(1), 2015 (b)(2), 2018 (b)(3) — renewed unchanged each cycle
  • 2021 (b)(6) — renewed expanded — adds "previously published musical works fixed in text or notation" (musical notation); terminology updated to "eligible person" (post-Marrakesh Treaty)
  • 2024 (b)(6) — renewed unchanged

Longest-running renewable exemption (eight consecutive cycles, 2003-2024). Note: in 2010 the Librarian of Congress GRANTED this exemption despite the Register recommending denial — the only known instance of such an override in this archive.

Motion pictures — captioning & audio description by educational disability services first granted 2018

Educational disability services may circumvent CSS/AACS/digital TPMs to caption/audio-describe motion pictures.

  • 2018 (b)(2) — new — students with disabilities only
  • 2021 (b)(2) — renewed expanded — beneficiaries expanded to students, faculty, OR staff with disabilities; "fair market price" standard; textbook-bundle scenario; explicit reuse permission; "accessible version of sufficient quality" definition added
  • 2024 (b)(2) — renewed unchanged
Motion pictures — R&D of accessibility players (blind/deaf/HoH) first granted 2012 · single cycle

Circumvent CSS/online-distribution TPMs for R&D of accessibility-enhanced players. Resulting player must not itself require circumvention.

  • 2012 (b)(8) — new
  • 2015+ — not renewed; likely no longer needed if resulting players did not require ongoing circumvention
Video games — accessibility input methods for physical disabilities first granted 2021 · lapsed 2024

Circumvent video-game TPMs (PC only) to enable alternative input methods (non-keyboard/non-mouse) for users with physical disabilities.

  • 2021 (b)(21) — new
  • 2024 — lapsed; NO renewal petition was filed (per 2024 Final Rule [PAGE 2] footnote 21). Not opposed, not denied — simply not petitioned.

Audiovisual Use (Criticism, Comment, Education) — Granted

Motion pictures — short clips for criticism, comment, & educational uses first granted 2006 · expanded every cycle

Make short clips from DVDs/Blu-ray/digital for criticism, comment, documentary, noncommercial video, e-book film analysis, K-12/college/MOOC/digital-literacy education.

  • 2006 (b)(1) — new — originally limited to film/media studies professors compiling classroom clips from DVDs
  • 2010 (b)(1) — expanded to three user categories: college/university educators, documentary filmmakers, noncommercial video creators
  • 2012 (b)(4)+(b)(5)+(b)(6)+(b)(7) — restructured into 4 sub-classes (DVD/online × full-circ/screen-cap); added K-12 educators and multimedia e-books — partial denial: short-clip circumvention for fictional filmmakers denied (noninfringing use not shown)
  • 2015 (b)(1) — consolidated back into single class with 8 enumerated sub-uses; added Blu-ray/AACS, MOOCs, K-12 students (screen-cap only), digital/media-literacy programs
  • 2018 (b)(1) — adds parody and biographical/historically-significant uses to documentary branch; allows K-12 students (under educator supervision) to use full circumvention
  • 2021 (b)(1) — adds "employees acting at the direction of faculty for the purpose of teaching a course" to both higher-ed and MOOC subparts
  • 2024 (b)(1) — renewed unchanged — partial denials: (1) OTW's proposed rewrite of noncommercial-videos subpart denied (no record/evidence); (2) MOOC expansion to for-profit/unaccredited entities denied (noninfringing use not shown)

Granted in every cycle since 2006 (seven consecutive cycles). The single most consistently broadened exemption in §1201 history.

Audiovisual Use — Denied Petitions

Space-shifting / format-shifting denied 2000-2021 (8 cycles)

Recurring proposal: permit users to shift lawfully-acquired works to alternative devices, formats, or platforms.

  • 2000 Proposal E.3 (partial) — denied — alternatives available (analog format)
  • 2003 Classes 8 + 15 — denied — space-shifting not shown to be noninfringing use
  • 2006 B.2 — denied — no legal precedent that space-shifting is noninfringing
  • 2010 IV.A.1 — denied — mere consumer inconvenience
  • 2012 Class 10 — denied — no adverse impact on noninfringing uses shown
  • 2015 Classes 8 + 10 — denied — "law of fair use, as it stands today, does not sanction broad-based space-shifting or format-shifting"
  • 2018 Class 3 — denied — no basis to depart from 2015 fair-use analysis
  • 2021 Class 6 — denied — petitioner failed to provide legal arguments or evidence
  • 2024 — not proposed

The most-denied proposal in §1201 history. The Register's position has been stable: in the absence of fair-use precedent recognizing space-shifting as noninfringing, §1201 cannot create one.

DVD region coding & OS-tethered playback (Linux DVDs) denied 2000-2010 (4 cycles)

Recurring proposal: permit circumvention of CSS to play DVDs on Linux or non-licensed platforms, or to bypass region coding.

  • 2000 Proposal E.3 — denied — matter of preference and inconvenience
  • 2003 Classes 11 + 16 — denied — options exist; mere inconvenience
  • 2006 B.3 + B.4 — denied as de minimis
  • 2010 IV.A.2 — denied — CSS-compatible Linux DVD players are in fact available
  • 2012+ — not proposed; Linux DVD playback effectively solved at the technical level
Backup copies of optical media (DVDs/CDs) denied 2003, 2006

Permit users to make backup copies of lawfully-acquired DVDs/CDs in case of physical damage.

  • 2003 Class 8 — denied — backup not shown to be a noninfringing use; DVDs not shown to be fragile
  • 2006 B.9 — denied — no legal authority that backup is noninfringing (apart from §117 for computer programs)
  • 2010+ — not proposed
Broadcast flag circumvention denied 2003, 2006, 2010

Permit circumvention of a (proposed/struck-down) FCC broadcast-flag mandate that would restrict downstream recording.

  • 2003 Class 20 — denied — broadcast flag still speculative
  • 2006 B.10 — denied — no broadcast flag mandate in effect
  • 2010 IV.D — denied — no DTV broadcast flag mandate; speculative

Tracks a particular regulatory fight from the 2000s. The FCC broadcast-flag rule was struck down by the D.C. Circuit in American Library Ass'n v. FCC (2005); the topic faded after 2010.

HDCP / HDMI / Blu-ray Image Constraint Token down-conversion denied 2010, 2018

Permit circumvention of HDCP/HDMI content-protection or Blu-ray ICT to obtain full-resolution playback/recording.

  • 2010 IV.E — denied — no evidence ICTs currently used on Blu-ray to restrict resolution
  • 2018 Class 4 — denied — record lacked detail and legal support; overly broad and de minimis impact

Distinct from the granted motion-pictures-clips class because the HDCP/HDMI proposal would permit full-resolution recording (not just short clips for criticism).

Livestream recording (audiovisual works) denied 2021 (procedural)

Permit circumvention of HTTP Live Streaming TPMs to record audiovisual livestreams.

  • 2021 Class 4 — denied procedurally — petitioner provided no legal arguments or evidence and did not participate in hearings
Motion picture clips in text messages denied 2021 (de minimis)

Permit circumvention to enable sending short motion-picture clips in text messages.

  • 2021 Class 2 — denied — de minimis showing; petitioner did not explain noninfringing-use theory
Copy-protected CDs & game-disc DRM (SecuROM/StarForce) denied 2003, 2006

Petitions for circumvention of audio CD TPMs (2003) and computer-game copy-protection (2006).

  • 2003 Class 18 — denied — deployment of CD TPMs has been minimal
  • 2006 B.6 — denied — no evidence adverse effect is the result of an access control

The Sony rootkit incident later got its own targeted GRANTED exemption in 2006 (cd-security-testing-2006), but the general "copy-protected CD" petitions for users were denied.

Research (TDM and Security) — Granted

Motion pictures — TDM research at nonprofit higher-ed first granted 2021

Higher-ed researchers may circumvent CSS/AACS/digital TPMs to deploy text-and-data-mining on a motion-picture corpus.

  • 2021 (b)(4) — new
  • 2024 (b)(4) — renewed expanded — viewing standard broadened from "verification of research findings" to "conduct TDM research or teaching"; trade-association disclosure right added; secure-connections + authenticated-credentials requirement added
Literary works — TDM research at nonprofit higher-ed first granted 2021

Higher-ed TDM research on literary-work corpora. Excludes computer programs and TDM-purpose compilations.

  • 2021 (b)(5) — new
  • 2024 (b)(5) — renewed expanded — same expansions as motion-pictures-tdm
Video games on PCs — security research 2010 only · precursor to general class

Circumvent video-game TPMs (on PCs) for good-faith security research.

  • 2010 (b)(4) — new
  • 2012+ — not renewed; subsumed into the broader good-faith security research class formalized in 2015
Good-faith security research (general) first granted 2015 · heavily expanded

Good-faith security research on any device/machine (and on systems/networks with owner authorization).

  • 2010 — precursor petitioned but denied: Halderman's broader security-testing class (covering sound recordings, audiovisual works, and non-game literary works) was DENIED as evidence-insufficient; only the narrow video-game-PC subset was granted that cycle
  • 2015 (b)(7) — new (general formulation) — initially limited to 3 device classes (consumer devices incl. voting machines; motorized land vehicles; implanted medical devices); 12-month delayed effective date
  • 2018 (b)(11) — renewed expanded — device-class limitation REMOVED; coverage added for computers/systems/networks with owner authorization; 12-month delay dropped
  • 2021 (b)(16) — renewed expanded — CFAA-non-violation precondition DROPPED; §1201 exemption no longer denied to researchers who incidentally violate CFAA (separate other-law liability remains possible)
  • 2024 (b)(18) — renewed unchanged

One of the most-expanded exemptions. Each renewal cycle from 2015 to 2021 substantively broadened the scope.

Sound recordings on CDs with security-flaw TPMs — 'Sony rootkit' testing 2006 only

Audio-CD security-flaw testing — direct response to the 2005 Sony BMG rootkit incident.

  • 2006 (b)(6) — new
  • 2010+ — not renewed; music industry abandoned CD-level TPMs; conceptually subsumed by general security-research class (2015+)

Research — Denied Petitions

Generative AI trustworthiness research denied 2024

Permit circumvention to enable trustworthiness research on generative AI systems (bias, hallucination, safety).

  • 2024 Class 4 — denied — adverse effects identified by proponents arise from third-party online-platform terms of service, not from §1201, so an exemption would not ameliorate their concerns

Notable because the Register's reasoning was structural (the harm arises from ToS, not from §1201) rather than evidentiary — i.e., even a perfect record would not have changed the outcome.

Encryption research (broader than §1201(g)) denied 2000, 2003

Broaden the encryption-research exemption beyond §1201(g)'s statutory carve-out.

  • 2000 Proposal E.6 — denied — not focused on a class of works
  • 2003 Class 22 — denied for insufficient evidence and failure to explain why existing exemptions are inadequate

Effectively subsumed in practice by the broad good-faith security-research class first granted in 2015.

Reverse engineering for interoperability (broader than §1201(f)) denied 2000, 2003

Broaden reverse-engineering beyond §1201(f)'s statutory carve-out (e.g., printer toner remanufacturing).

  • 2000 Proposal E.5 — denied — class cannot be defined by purpose
  • 2003 Class 21 — denied — §1201(f) is adequate; addresses remanufacturers' concerns
Forensic investigator evidence collection denied 2010

Permit forensic investigators to circumvent TPMs to collect digital evidence.

  • 2010 IV.C — denied — proponent failed to describe the class of works or provide compelling evidence

Preservation by Libraries / Archives / Museums — Granted

Motion pictures (DVD/Blu-ray) — preservation by LAMs first granted 2021

Eligible LAMs may circumvent CSS/AACS on damaged or deteriorating DVDs/Blu-rays when no fairly-priced replacement or LAM-accessible streaming alternative exists.

  • 2021 (b)(3) — new
  • 2024 (b)(3) — renewed unchanged
Software preservation by LAMs (non-game programs) first granted 2018 · expanded twice

Eligible LAMs may circumvent TPMs on lawfully acquired non-game programs no longer commercially available, for lawful preservation.

  • 2018 (b)(13) — new — on-premises only
  • 2021 (b)(18) — renewed expanded — adds offsite electronic distribution to ONE USER AT A TIME, for a limited time, where LAM has no notice of non-private-study use
  • 2024 (b)(20) — renewed expanded — adds explicit "one user at a time" definition clarifying that a LAM with multiple lawfully owned copies may lend different copies to different users simultaneously — partial denial: the proposal to REMOVE the single-user limitation entirely was rejected (noninfringing use not shown)
Video games — preservation when server abandoned (+ offline games by LAMs from 2018) first granted 2015 · expanded 2018

When authentication server abandoned, individuals may restore local gameplay on PC/console; LAMs may preserve.

  • 2015 (b)(8) — new — only server-failure cases
  • 2018 (b)(12) — renewed expanded — adds subpart (ii) for LAM preservation of offline games no longer commercially available; "complete games" definition expanded for LAM preservation to include hybrid local/server games
  • 2021 (b)(17) — renewed unchanged — partial denial: Class 14(b) proposed off-premises LAM access to preserved video games — denied (noninfringing use not shown)
  • 2024 (b)(19) — renewed unchanged — partial denial: Class 6(b) again proposed removing the on-premises limitation — denied (market harm concerns)

Preservation — Denied Petitions

DRM authentication server shutdown (broad — beyond video games) denied 2010

General DRM server shutdown across all works. The narrower video-game-specific version was later granted in 2015.

  • 2010 IV.B.1 + IV.B.2 — denied — proponent did not sustain burden of showing adverse effect
  • 2015 — narrower video-game version GRANTED this cycle as video-games-preservation (b)(8)

Conceptual precursor to the granted video-games-preservation class — the broad cross-work proposal was denied, but a narrower video-game-server-shutdown petition succeeded five years later.

Library-distributed audiobooks (electronic audio format) denied 2006

Permit library users to circumvent TPMs on electronically-distributed audiobooks.

  • 2006 B.7 — denied — complaint was about software incompatibility, not access controls; the technology in question was held to be "use-facilitating" rather than "access-controlling"

Wireless Network Connectivity

Wireless devices — unlocking to connect to a different carrier first granted 2006 · narrowed twice, expanded three times

Unlock a wireless device to switch carrier networks, where new network operator authorizes the connection.

  • 2006 (b)(5) — new — cellphone handsets only
  • 2010 (b)(3) — renewed narrowed — restricted to "used" handsets (previously activated on a network)
  • 2012 (b)(3) — renewed narrowed — only handsets acquired within 90 days of the rule's effective date; carrier-failure-to-unlock condition
  • 2015 (b)(3) — renewed expanded — device classes broadened: cellphones, tablets, mobile hotspots, wearables (all "used")
  • 2018 (b)(5) — renewed expanded — "used device" requirement DROPPED
  • 2021 (b)(8) — renewed expanded — device-class enumeration DROPPED; any wireless device qualifies
  • 2024 (b)(8) — renewed unchanged

Particularly complex history: narrowed twice under industry pressure (2010, 2012), then broadened three consecutive times (2015, 2018, 2021). Mirrors the Unlocking Consumer Choice and Wireless Competition Act of 2014.

Interoperability / Software Installation (Jailbreaking) — Granted

Smartphones & portable mobile devices — jailbreaking (+ removal of software) first granted 2010 · the 'iPhone class'

Install lawfully obtained third-party apps; from 2015 also remove manufacturer/carrier software.

  • 2010 (b)(2) — new — the original 'iPhone class'; handsets only, interoperability only
  • 2012 (b)(2) — renewed unchanged; the Register declined to extend to tablets this cycle — partial denial: tablet expansion proposal denied (category too "broad and ill-defined") — tablet expansion later granted in 2015
  • 2015 (b)(4) — renewed expanded — adds "portable all-purpose mobile computing devices" (tablet-like) AND adds removal-of-software clause
  • 2018 (b)(6), 2021 (b)(9), 2024 (b)(9) — renewed unchanged
Smart televisions — jailbreaking (interoperability only) first granted 2015

Install lawfully obtained third-party apps on smart TVs. NO removal-of-software clause (narrower than smartphone class by design).

  • 2015 (b)(5) — new
  • 2018 (b)(7) — renewed unchanged
  • 2021 (b)(10) — renewed expanded — "smart televisions" definition broadened to include devices physically separate from a TV whose primary purpose is to stream authorized video (set-top streaming boxes like Roku, Apple TV, Chromecast)
  • 2024 (b)(10) — renewed unchanged
Voice assistant devices — jailbreaking (+ removal of software) first granted 2018

Install lawfully obtained third-party apps on voice-input devices (smart speakers).

  • 2018 (b)(8) — new
  • 2021 (b)(11), 2024 (b)(11) — renewed unchanged
Routers & dedicated network devices — jailbreaking (interoperability only) first granted 2021

Install third-party firmware (OpenWrt, DD-WRT, etc.) on routers and dedicated network devices.

  • 2021 (b)(12) — new
  • 2024 (b)(12) — renewed unchanged

Jailbreaking — Denied Petitions

Video game console jailbreaking denied 2012, 2015

Permit jailbreaking of video game consoles for software interoperability (parallel to the granted smartphone class).

  • 2012 Class 3 — denied — evidentiary record did not support adverse impact
  • 2015 Class 19 — denied — "jailbreaking of video game consoles continues to be closely associated with video game piracy"

The Register's piracy-correlation reasoning has been consistent. The 2021/2024 codified consumer-device repair class includes a narrow video-game-console carve-out for optical-drive repair only, not for jailbreaking.

E-book reader jailbreaking denied 2015

Permit jailbreaking of dedicated e-book readers (Kindle, Nook, etc.) for software interoperability.

  • 2015 Class 18 — denied — insufficient factual or legal support
Personal computers / OS-restricted programs / secure boot denied 2006, 2012

Circumvent access controls on general-purpose personal computers, OS-locked computer programs, or secure-boot mechanisms.

  • 2006 B.5 — denied — insufficient evidence
  • 2012 Class 4 — denied — speculative; no specific compelling evidence

PC jailbreaking has effectively never been on the table — partly because most PCs don't enforce OS lock-in the way mobile devices do.

Repair, Diagnosis, & Modification — Granted

Motorized land vehicles (+ marine vessels from 2021) — diagnosis, repair, lawful modification first granted 2015 · right-to-repair anchor

Owner repair/diagnosis/modification of vehicle ECU/computer programs. Subscription-service programs excluded.

  • 2015 (b)(6) — new — 12-month delayed effective date; broad telematics/entertainment carve-out
  • 2018 (b)(9) — renewed expanded — 12-month delay DROPPED; telematics/entertainment exclusion replaced with narrower subscription-service exclusion
  • 2021 (b)(13) — renewed expanded — adds marine vessels (boats, commercial vessels, mechanized agricultural vessels)
  • 2024 (b)(13) — renewed unchanged
Vehicles & marine vessels — owner access to operational/diagnostic/telematics data first granted 2024

Owners/lessees (or agents) may circumvent to access, store, and share vehicle/vessel operational data.

  • 2024 (b)(14) — new — distinct from (b)(13) repair class
Consumer devices — diagnosis, maintenance, or repair first granted 2018 · broadened 2021

Right-to-repair for consumer devices.

  • 2018 (b)(10) — new — limited to "smartphone or home appliance or home system" (refrigerator, thermostat, HVAC, electrical system)
  • 2021 (b)(14) — renewed expanded — broadened to any "device primarily designed for use by consumers"; narrow video-game-console carve-out added (optical-drive repair only, must restore TPMs)
  • 2024 (b)(15) — renewed unchanged — partial denial: a proposed broader expansion to all commercial and industrial equipment was rejected; only retail food-prep equipment was carved out and granted as the separately-tracked (b)(16)
Medical devices & systems — diagnosis, maintenance, or repair first granted 2021

Independent repair of lawfully acquired medical devices/systems and related data files.

  • 2021 (b)(15) — new
  • 2024 (b)(17) — renewed unchanged
Retail commercial food prep equipment — diagnosis, maintenance, or repair first granted 2024

Repair of commercial food-prep equipment (e.g., the McDonald's ice cream machine problem).

  • 2024 (b)(16) — new — narrow carve-out from a broader proposed Class 5 (the broader scope was denied)

Repair — Denied Petitions

Commercial & industrial equipment repair (beyond food prep) denied 2024

Repair of all software-enabled commercial and industrial equipment.

  • 2024 Class 5 (broader scope) — denied — insufficient evidence on record beyond the food-prep carve-out

Likely to recur in future cycles as right-to-repair advocacy expands beyond consumer devices.

Patient / Owner Data Access

Medical-device data — patient access to own data first granted 2015 · major 2021 expansion

Patients (or agents from 2021) may circumvent to access data their own medical devices generate.

  • 2015 (b)(10) — new (Hugo Campos's class) — implanted devices only; passive monitoring of wireless transmissions only; patient personally must undertake circumvention
  • 2018 (b)(4) — renewed unchanged
  • 2021 (b)(7) — renewed expanded — three expansions: "by or on behalf of" a patient (delegation permitted); implantation requirement REMOVED; passive-monitoring restriction REMOVED
  • 2024 (b)(7) — renewed unchanged

Material / Feedstock Interoperability

3D printers — feedstock / material interoperability first granted 2015 · expanded twice

Circumvent TPMs that limit a 3D printer to manufacturer-approved feedstock/material.

  • 2015 (b)(9) — new — initially limited to "microchip-reliant" TPMs and excluded 3D printers producing regulated-commerce goods
  • 2018 (b)(14) — renewed expanded — regulated-commerce exclusion DROPPED
  • 2021 (b)(19) — renewed expanded — "microchip-reliant" qualifier DROPPED; "feedstock" generalized to "material"
  • 2024 (b)(21) — renewed unchanged

Software License Compliance

FOSS programs — investigation of potential license infringement first granted 2021

Investigate FOSS license violations (typically GPL compliance) on a lawfully acquired device. Excludes video game consoles. Standing required.

  • 2021 (b)(20) — new
  • 2024 (b)(22) — renewed unchanged

Denied — Class-Definition Disputes & Threshold Issues

Broad use-based exemptions ('all fair use', 'all noninfringing uses') denied 2000-2012 (4 cycles)

Recurring proposal for a blanket exemption covering all works for noninfringing or fair uses.

  • 2000 Proposals E.7 + E.9 — denied — "a particular class of work must be determined primarily by reference to qualities of the work itself. It cannot be defined by reference to the class of users or uses of the work."
  • 2003 Classes 1, 2, 4, 7 — denied procedurally — "utterly failed to propose a particular class of copyrighted works"
  • 2006 B.8 — denied procedurally — failed to articulate a cognizable class
  • 2012 Class 1 — denied — beyond scope of rulemaking

Petitioned and denied in four cycles. After 2012, sophisticated petitioners stopped framing proposals this way — though the recurring petitions before that reflected a persistent advocacy strategy that simply did not match the statute.

Public-domain works behind TPMs denied 2003, 2006, 2012

Petition to permit circumvention of TPMs on works in the public domain.

  • 2003 Class 7 — denied — §1201 not applicable to public-domain works; no exemption needed
  • 2006 A.2-secondary — denied — not access controls; no exemption needed
  • 2012 Class 1 — denied — beyond scope of rulemaking

The Register's position is doctrinally clean: §1201 only prohibits circumventing TPMs that effectively control access to copyrighted works. But the practical problem — TPMs sometimes do lock up public-domain content as a side effect of locking up copyrighted content — has not been addressed in the rulemaking.

Avionics / aircraft data systems denied 2018

Permit circumvention of access controls on aircraft electronic systems to enable access to flight/operations data.

  • 2018 Class 11 — denied — the data at issue (flight stats, fuel economy, etc.) are facts, which are not copyrightable, so §1201 does not apply

One historical note. In 2010 the Librarian of Congress overrode the Register on the literary-works-accessibility class — the Register recommended denial but the Librarian granted it anyway (the only known instance of such an override in this archive).
